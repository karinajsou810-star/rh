The challenge

A routine that could quickly become overwhelming.

Managing the agenda, coordinating travel, organizing events, handling suppliers, and keeping up with the day to day of a busy family, all at the same time, can quickly become overwhelming. Every request felt urgent, because nothing was planned far enough ahead to be anything else.

Trips were booked at the last minute, at last-minute prices. Events landed on top of already full weeks. The agenda lived in three places at once. And the small details that make a day run smoothly depended entirely on someone remembering them.

It is not a matter of effort. It is what happens when a demanding routine has no dedicated support behind it.

"You would only notice the amount of work behind that routine when something slipped. And with Karina, things simply stopped slipping."
Ticiana Schultz · Personal assistant, executive office
The solution

Dedicated support, so the day to day simply flows.

That is where lifestyle management comes in. Hospitality teaches you to see a request before it is spoken, and years of personal assistance turn that instinct into a profession: one trusted person taking ownership of the personal side, end to end.

One calendar became the single source of truth, with priorities protected and breathing room built in. Travel turned into a door-to-door service: flights, transfers, stays, and a plan B for the steps that usually go wrong. Events were delivered calmly, with suppliers compared, budgets tracked, and checklists owned, so the hosts could simply be present and enjoy them.

And the same care went into the home: routines organized, suppliers coordinated, everything in its place. A well-run home makes everything else lighter. For almost eight years, the routine ran on structure instead of urgency, and the office got back the one thing no one can buy more of: time.

"Karina ran the personal side of a demanding routine for years, with total discretion. Agenda, trips, events, the house: if she was responsible for it, you did not have to think about it again."
Benedita Jerônimo · Operations professional
